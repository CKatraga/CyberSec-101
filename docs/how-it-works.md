# How this course works

You learn by **doing**, not by memorizing. Every topic follows the same rhythm.

## The loop

1. **Watch** the short video(s) at the top of a module. Don't take heavy notes — just watch.
2. **Do** the lab. Type every command yourself. Break things. That's the point.
3. **Capture** the result — a CTF flag, a screenshot, or a working script.
4. **Record** it: tick the box in the [progress tracker](progress.md) and write ~3 lines in your [journal](journal.md) (what you did, what confused you, one thing you learned).
5. **Check in** with your mentor. Show the flag/screenshot. Ask your questions.

## Rules that make this work

!!! success "Do"
    - Type commands by hand instead of copy-pasting, at least the first time.
    - When something errors, **read the error out loud** before asking. 80% of the time the fix is in the message.
    - Keep a running list of "commands I learned" — it becomes your personal cheat-sheet.
    - Timebox frustration: stuck for **20 minutes** → write down what you tried, then ask.

!!! danger "Don't"
    - Don't peek at lab `setup.sh` files — that's the answer key.
    - Don't run attack tools against anything you don't own or aren't given permission to test. In this course you only ever attack **your own** containers and **intentionally vulnerable practice apps**. That line is the whole ethics of the field.

## What you need

- A laptop with a browser. That's genuinely enough for Modules 1–6 (we use a free cloud Linux workspace).
- Later (Module 7+) you'll install **Kali Linux** in a VM on your own machine — Pranith will help you set that up.
- A GitHub account (free). [Setup instructions here](setup/codespaces.md).

## Time budget

Plan for **~5–7 hours/week**: roughly 2 hours of video and 3–5 hours of hands-on. The course is designed as **16 weeks**, but go at your pace — depth beats speed.
